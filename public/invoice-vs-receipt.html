Invoice vs Receipt: What is the Difference?

Published by Make Invoice Online Editorial Team

For freelancers, contractors, and small business owners, understanding the distinct accounting roles of an invoice and a receipt is essential for maintaining clean financial records and tax compliance. While both documents track financial transactions, they serve completely different purposes in the sales process.

What is an Invoice?

An invoice is a commercial request for payment sent by a seller or service provider to a client before the payment is made. It details the goods or services rendered, total balance due, tax calculations, and payment terms (such as Net 30).

What is a Receipt?

A receipt is an official proof of transaction issued after a client has completed their payment. It acts as documentation showing that the obligation stated in an invoice has been fulfilled.

Key Differences at a Glance

  • Timing: Invoices are sent before payment; receipts are issued after payment.
  • Purpose: Invoices request payment; receipts confirm payment execution.
  • Key Details: Invoices specify due dates and payment methods, whereas receipts list transaction approval numbers and payment dates.
